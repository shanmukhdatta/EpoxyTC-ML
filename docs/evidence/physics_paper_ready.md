# Thermal Conductivity of Particle-Filled Epoxy: Experimental and Machine-Learning Assessment Across Three Regimes — Conditional physics evidence

The Maxwell–Eucken, symmetric Bruggeman, spherical Hamilton–Crosser, Lewis–Nielsen and Hashin–Shtrikman expressions were evaluated with published experimental data and explicitly classified proxy constituent parameters. An independent epoxy composite containing 32 vol% negative-thermal-expansion nanoparticles (DOI: 10.1177/0021998310385031) reports composite conductivities of **0.280 W/(m·K) at 80 K** and **0.480 W/(m·K) at 298 K**, with neat-epoxy conductivities of 0.070 and 0.170 W/(m·K), respectively. We used a literature-supported **assumed** nanoparticle conductivity of 2.75 W/(m·K) as a common scenario at both temperatures; the exact filler temperature dependence remains unverified. Predicted Maxwell–Eucken conductivities were **0.1586 and 0.3559 W/(m·K)**, compared with **0.3011 and 0.5005 W/(m·K)** from symmetric Bruggeman. The Bruggeman absolute percentage errors were **7.53% and 4.26%**, respectively; Maxwell–Eucken errors were **43.35% and 25.84%**. Both reported measurements lay within the conditional Hashin–Shtrikman bounds. Hamilton–Crosser with spherical shape factor is algebraically identical to Maxwell–Eucken.

**Historical generic-matrix-only scenario (retained, superseded as the final manuscript interpretation):** As a separate physical plausibility assessment, we approximately digitized **30 markers** in the authors' manuscript Figure 3 (epoxy–SS304 and epoxy–Al2O3, 4.2/300/323 K, five volume fractions). For 300 K and 5–15 vol% filler, the graph readings range from **1.186 to 1.517 W/(m·K)**. With a **non-specimen-specific proxy** neat-epoxy conductivity of 0.217 W/(m·K), the ideal isotropic spherical Maxwell upper limit is at most **0.332 W/(m·K)** over these fractions. This discrepancy reflects the mismatch between the idealized model, proxy constituents and manuscript values. It is **not proof of experimental error**. The ±0.04 W/(m·K) digitization tolerance is **not experimental uncertainty**. No numeric apparatus-specific GUM budget is claimed without heater-power, heat-loss, geometry, calibration and repeatability records.

Seven other candidate source reconstructions and two epoxy/diamond/graphene cases are **not fully authenticated original CSV observations**. Values from these cases must not be described as independent validation of the original 256-row dataset. For publishing, cite the primary specimen paper and the actual external source of every proxy property, including its temperature and material-grade limitations.

## Important updated 300 K input sensitivity result

The revised manuscript additionally uses 1.064 W/(m·K) from a separate published epoxy formulation (Kumar et al., *Cryogenics* 144 (2024), 103973) versus an earlier 0.200 W/(m·K) generic matrix proxy. Ten approximate manuscript SS304/Al2O3 graph-read markers yield conditional Maxwell graph-read mean absolute percentage deviations of **80.46%** and **1.43%**, respectively; Bruggeman using the cross-study 1.064 matrix gives **4.89%**. These values describe **assumed-input sensitivity only**, not independently authenticated experimental accuracy or batch identity. The assumed bulk SS304 and Al2O3 conductivity proxies are likewise not powder-grade verified.
